# The 2026 cars and rules

## What changed in 2026
2026 is the biggest rules reset in years. The cars are smaller and lighter, the power unit has a far bigger electric part, and the Drag Reduction System (DRS) is gone, replaced by active aerodynamics and a new Overtake Mode. A new team, Cadillac, joined, and new engine makers Audi and the Red Bull-Ford partnership arrived. Be aware that many older videos and articles describe the pre-2026 rules.

## Is DRS still used in 2026
No. DRS, the rear-wing flap used to help overtaking, has been removed for 2026. Overtaking help now comes from active aerodynamics, which every driver can use in set zones, and from Overtake Mode, which gives extra electrical power to a car that is close enough behind another car.

## The 2026 power unit
The power unit is still a 1.6 litre V6 turbo petrol engine plus a hybrid system, but the electrical side is about three times more powerful than before. The MGU-K now delivers up to 350 kW, and the output is split roughly equally between the combustion engine and the electric motor. The MGU-H, which recovered energy from exhaust heat, has been removed to simplify the unit.

## Sustainable fuel
In 2026 all cars run on 100 percent advanced sustainable fuel, which is made from non-fossil sources such as waste or captured carbon. This is part of F1's goal of net zero carbon by 2030.

## Active aerodynamics
The front and rear wings now have movable parts that change position during a lap. In Corner Mode, the flaps are closed to give maximum downforce and grip through the corners. In Straight Mode, the flaps open to reduce drag, and increase top speed on a straight. Before the season these were called Z-mode and X-mode. Drivers open the wings on the straight sections of a lap (called zones), and they close again when the driver brakes or lifts off the throttle.

## Straight Mode and Corner Mode
Corner Mode is the normal, high-downforce state of the wings, good for corners. Straight Mode is the low-drag state used in designated straight-line zones. Unlike DRS, any driver can use Straight Mode in the zones, with no need to be close to another car.

## Overtake Mode
Overtake Mode is the new overtaking aid for 2026. If a driver is within one second of the car ahead at the single detection point on the lap, which is usually the last corner, they get Overtake Mode on the following lap. It gives them extra electric energy (an extra 0.5 megajoules of recharge) and a stronger power delivery for longer, so they can attack. It can be used anywhere on the track, all at once or spread across a lap, which is different from DRS, which only worked in set zones.

## Boost Mode
Boost is a button the driver can press at any time to deploy the maximum electrical power, for attack or defence. It uses stored battery energy, so using it too often can leave a car short of power later in the lap.

## Recharge and super clipping
Because the battery is so important, cars constantly harvest energy. They do it under braking, at part throttle, by lifting early before corners (lift and coast), and by a technique called super clipping, where the car recovers energy at the end of a long straight while the driver is still at full throttle. This can make a car look like it is slowing slightly at the end of a straight, which is normal and not a fault.

## Why cars seem to slow at the end of straights
In 2026 the combustion engine and battery share the work, so at the end of long straights the car may stop accelerating as hard while it recharges. Viewers sometimes mistake this for a problem. It is a deliberate energy-management choice, sometimes called super clipping.

## Lighter and smaller cars
The 2026 cars are smaller and lighter than the 2022 to 2025 generation, which makes them more agile in corners. The beam wing at the rear has been removed and the wings are narrower, with cleaner endplates. They also produce less dirty air, which should help cars follow each other more closely.

## Who builds the engines in 2026
Mercedes supplies Mercedes, McLaren, Williams and Alpine. Ferrari supplies Ferrari, Haas and Cadillac. Honda supplies Aston Martin. Audi builds its own power unit for its works team. Red Bull Powertrains, working with Ford, supplies Red Bull Racing and Racing Bulls.

## What is a works team versus a customer team
A works team builds its own engine, such as Ferrari or Mercedes. A customer team buys engines from another manufacturer, such as Haas buying from Ferrari. Some teams, like Aston Martin with Honda, have a close partnership with one supplier.

## Cadillac, the 11th team
Cadillac joined the grid in 2026 as the eleventh team, which brought the field to 22 cars. It uses Ferrari engines to start with, and plans to build its own power unit later in the decade.

## Qualifying with 22 cars
Because there are now 22 cars, the bottom six cars are eliminated in each of Q1 and Q2, instead of the bottom five when there were 20 cars. Q3 still has the top 10.

## Car names like W17
Each team gives its car a model name that changes every year. For example, the 2026 Mercedes is called the W17. The name has no effect on how it races, but you will see it on screen and in commentary.
