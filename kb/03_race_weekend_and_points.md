# Race weekend and points

## The 2026 grid at a glance
The 2026 season has 11 teams with two drivers each, so 22 cars, and a calendar of 24 races. The teams are Alpine, Aston Martin, Audi, Cadillac, Ferrari, Haas, McLaren, Mercedes, Racing Bulls, Red Bull Racing and Williams. Cadillac is the new 11th team, and Audi has taken over what used to be the Sauber team.

## Standard race weekend schedule
A normal grand prix weekend has three one-hour practice sessions (two on Friday, one on Saturday), then qualifying on Saturday, then the race on Sunday. Practice is for testing setups and tyres, qualifying sets the starting grid, and the race gives the points.

## Free practice (FP1, FP2, FP3)
Free practice sessions are one hour each. Teams test car setups, try different tyre compounds, practise pit stops and run long stints to learn how the tyres wear. Lap times in practice do not matter much, since teams run with different fuel loads and engine settings, so do not read too much into who is fastest.

## Qualifying format (Q1, Q2, Q3)
Qualifying is a knockout session that sets the grid for the race. With 22 cars in 2026, Q1 lasts 18 minutes and the slowest six cars (17th to 22nd) are eliminated. Q2 lasts 15 minutes and the next six slowest (11th to 16th) are out. Q3 lasts 13 minutes, and the remaining 10 drivers fight for pole position and the top 10 grid spots. Each session starts fresh, with no lap times carried forward.

## Why qualifying matters
Overtaking is difficult at many circuits, so where you start strongly affects where you finish. That is why teams care so much about the single fastest lap in qualifying, and why pole position is valuable.

## Sprint weekend format
At six race weekends a season, F1 runs a sprint weekend. On Friday there is one practice session followed by Sprint Qualifying. On Saturday the Sprint race is held first, and then normal qualifying for the Sunday grand prix. Sunday is the full grand prix as usual. Sprint weekends have less practice time, so teams have less chance to learn the car.

## Sprint Qualifying
Sprint Qualifying is a shorter version of qualifying that sets the grid for the Sprint race only. It has three parts: SQ1 of 12 minutes, SQ2 of 10 minutes and SQ3 of 8 minutes. There are rules about which tyres must be used: medium tyres in SQ1 and SQ2, and soft tyres in SQ3.

## Sprint race
The Sprint is a short race of about 100 km, roughly a third of a grand prix and around 30 minutes long. There is no mandatory pit stop, so most drivers run the whole race on one set of tyres. Only the top eight score points: 8 for the winner, then 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1.

## Does the Sprint decide the Sunday grid?
No. The grid for Sunday's grand prix is set by normal qualifying on Saturday, separate from the Sprint result. Sprint points still count in the championship.

## Race distance and time limit
A grand prix is the smallest number of laps that go beyond 305 km. Monaco is shorter, at 260 km, because the track is slow and tight. A race has a maximum time of two hours, and if it is stopped with a red flag the total limit becomes three hours. Most races last around 1.5 to 2 hours.

## Mandatory tyre rule
In a dry grand prix, each driver must use at least two different dry tyre compounds, which means at least one pit stop. If it rains and wet or intermediate tyres are used, this requirement does not apply. In the Sprint there is no mandatory stop.

## How the race starts
Cars leave the pits for a formation lap, then line up in their grid slots. Five red lights come on one by one, and when they all go out the race starts. Starts are standing starts, with the cars stationary on the grid. A jump start is penalised.

## Championship points for a grand prix
The top 10 finishers score points: 25 for first, 18 for second, 15 for third, 12 for fourth, 10 for fifth, 8 for sixth, 6 for seventh, 4 for eighth, 2 for ninth and 1 for tenth. There is no point for the fastest lap any more. If a race is stopped very early and cannot be restarted, a reduced points scale applies.

## Drivers' versus Constructors' titles
Two championships run at once. The Drivers' Championship adds up each driver's points. The Constructors' Championship adds the points of both drivers of each team. A driver can win the title even if their team does not win the constructors' one.

## When the race ends
The race ends when the leader takes the chequered flag after completing the full number of laps, or when the time limit is reached. Other cars finish when they cross the line after the leader. Drivers do a cool-down lap, park in the parc fermé area, and the top three go to the podium. Results can still change after the race if the stewards add penalties or cars fail technical checks.

## Provisional results
Right after the race, the results are provisional. Time penalties given during or after the race are applied to the finishing times, which can change positions. The result becomes final after checks and any protest period.
