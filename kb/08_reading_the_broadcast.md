# Reading the live broadcast

## The timing tower
The timing tower is the list on the side of the screen showing each driver in race order. Each line normally shows the position, a three-letter driver code, the team colour, the tyre type the driver is using, and the time gap. It updates all the time, so watch it to see who is gaining or losing places.

## Three-letter driver codes
Drivers are shown with three-letter abbreviations, usually the first three letters of their surname, such as VER for Verstappen. A few are different, so check the full name when a broadcast first shows it.

## Gap to leader versus interval
Some graphics show the gap to the race leader, others show the interval to the car just ahead. If you see a small interval like +0.8, that car is very close to the one in front. In 2026 an interval below one second at the detection point lets a driver use Overtake Mode on the next lap.

## Tyre icons and tyre age
Next to each driver you may see a coloured circle: red for Soft, yellow for Medium, white for Hard, green for Intermediate and blue for Wet. A number may show how many laps old that set of tyres is. Older tyres usually mean a pit stop is coming soon.

## Pit stop count
Some graphics show the number of pit stops a driver has made so far. A driver with fewer stops may be on a longer strategy and could pit later.

## Purple, green and yellow lap times
Purple means the fastest time anyone has set so far in the session. Green is a personal best for that driver. Yellow means slower than their best. Sector times appear in the same colours, so a "purple sector" means the fastest anyone has gone in that part of the track.

## Fastest lap graphic
When a driver sets the fastest lap of the race, the graphic shows purple. In the race, it does not earn a point any more, but it is still a nice stat, and often a sign that a driver on fresh tyres is pushing hard.

## What PIT, OUT and DNF mean on screen
PIT means a driver is in the pit lane, and OUT usually means retired or out of the session. Some graphics also show DNF or a red cross for retirement.

## On-screen flags and banners
When a flag or control message appears on screen, like yellow, SC or VSC, it matches the situations described in the flags guide. A yellow banner often shows the sector. SC means Safety Car, VSC means Virtual Safety Car, and a red banner means the race has been stopped.

## Team radio
Team radio messages are short conversations between the driver and the pit wall. Terms you will hear include "box, box" (pit now), "push" (drive faster), "manage" (save tyres or energy), "lift and coast" (lift early before braking to save energy or fuel), and "stay out" (do not pit yet).

## What a gap closing really means
If the gap to the car ahead is dropping by a few tenths each lap, the driver behind is faster, which usually leads to an attack in a few laps. In traffic or dirty air, though, a faster car can still struggle to pass.

## Why a driver slows on the straight
If a car looks slow or you see a car move off the racing line, it may be because of a yellow flag, a Safety Car, or a driver managing energy or tyres. Look for a banner on screen or listen to the commentators.

## Battles and defence
A driver who is being chased may defend by taking the inside line into a corner, but must leave space and can only move once in reaction to the attacker. Commentators describe attacks with phrases like "getting a run" or "using the tow", meaning the car behind is getting a speed advantage from the slipstream.

## Weather and the radar
If it rains, the TV graphics may show a rain radar and the track status. Teams look at the radar to decide whether to switch to Intermediate tyres, which is one of the biggest strategy calls in a race.
