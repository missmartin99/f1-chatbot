# Flags and signals

## Green flag
A green flag means the track is clear and racing or normal running can resume. It is shown at the end of a yellow flag zone, and signals the hazard is behind you.

## Yellow flag (single)
A single waved yellow flag warns of danger on or beside the track in that sector, such as a stopped car or debris. Drivers must slow down and be ready to change direction, and overtaking is not allowed in the yellow zone until they pass the green flag.

## Double yellow flag
Two waved yellow flags mean a serious hazard, such as a car blocking part of the track or marshals working on it. Drivers must slow down significantly and be prepared to stop if needed. No overtaking is allowed.

## Yellow flag speed rules (2026 guidelines)
The 2026 driving guidelines tell drivers they must show they slowed enough under yellow flags. A suggested benchmark is a sector time at least 5 percent slower under a single waved yellow, and at least 15 percent slower under double waved yellows. In qualifying, drivers are expected to abandon their lap if a yellow flag is waved in their sector. A car losing control under yellows is usually investigated.

## Blue flag
A blue flag tells a driver that a faster car is about to lap them and they must let it through at the next safe opportunity. It is used for lapped cars in a race. Ignoring blue flags repeatedly, commonly three times, can lead to a penalty.

## Red flag
A red flag means the session or race is stopped, because of a serious crash, debris, dangerous weather or anything making racing unsafe. Cars slow down and return to the pit lane. The pit exit is closed with a red light, and the cars queue there. Teams can repair cars and change tyres while stopped.

## Black flag
A black flag, shown with the driver's car number, means disqualification: the driver must return to the pits and is out of the session. It is rare and used for serious rule breaches or ignoring instructions.

## Black flag with orange disc
A black flag with an orange circle, shown with the car number, tells a driver that their car has a mechanical problem that could be dangerous, such as loose bodywork or a fluid leak. They must pit so it can be fixed.

## Black and white flag (diagonal)
A black and white diagonal flag is a warning for unsportsmanlike driving, F1's version of a yellow card. It is shown with the driver's car number. It is also commonly used after repeated track limits breaches. Further offences can lead to a penalty.

## White flag
A white flag warns that there is a slow-moving vehicle ahead on the track, such as a recovery vehicle, medical car or a car with a problem. It does not mean the last lap in Formula 1, even though some other racing series use it that way.

## Yellow and red striped flag
A yellow flag with red vertical stripes warns of a slippery surface ahead, such as oil, water or loose gravel. It is held still, not waved. It does not require slowing down like the yellow flag, but drivers should be careful.

## Chequered flag
The black and white chequered flag signals the end of the session or race. It is waved at the finish line as each car completes its final lap. After the flag, drivers do a cool-down lap and return to the pits.

## Flag lights and panels
Marshal posts around the circuit have light panels that show the same signals as flags, and the steering wheel on every car also shows warnings. The electronic lights and physical flags mean exactly the same thing, and both are used at every grand prix.

## Race start lights
Five red lights come on one by one above the grid. When all five go out, the race starts. A driver who moves before the lights go out has jumped the start and faces a penalty, usually a 10 second penalty.

## Pit exit lights
At the end of the pit lane, a traffic light shows red when cars must not leave, such as during a red flag, a race start, or when a car is coming at speed on the track. Green means pit exit is open.

## Safety Car lights
The Safety Car has its orange lights on while it is deployed. If the lights turn green, lapped cars may overtake to rejoin the back of the queue. When the lights are switched off, it means the Safety Car will come into the pits at the end of that lap and racing restarts.
